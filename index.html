Chapter One

You Were So Small.

so small, so special 🌸

And I still remember you.

Not because I knew what the future would look like. I didn't.

I just remember a little girl who had something quietly special about her.

Maybe it was your innocence. Maybe your little ways. Maybe it was simply the feeling that this child would leave a memory behind.

I never imagined that years later, I would stand in front of you as your teacher — and feel grateful that life somehow brought that little girl back into my story.

And then life moved on...

Years passed quietly.

I finished Class 12. I left that chapter of my own school life behind and eventually returned to the same world — this time as a teacher.

And then I saw you again.

For a moment, it felt like time had folded in on itself.

The little girl I remembered was suddenly standing in front of me again.

student & teacher 📚

And suddenly, the memory became real.

You were my student.

I thought I would simply teach you, explain things, correct mistakes, give homework and watch you learn.

I didn't know that somewhere between all those ordinary days, you would become one of the students I would remember long after the lesson ended.

And yes... somehow you also brought a lot of laughter and chaos with you. 😂

Chapter Three

Somewhere Along The Way, Everything Changed.

At first, it really was just teacher and student.

Lessons became conversations.

Conversations became laughter.

Laughter became memories.

And memories became something I will always treasure.

Somewhere between the questions, the random talks, the serious moments and the ridiculous ones, I realized that teaching you wasn't only about what I could teach you.

You were teaching me something too.

What it feels like when a student trusts you, respects you, remembers you, and makes you feel that your presence actually mattered.

Of course... it wasn't all emotional. 😂

Chapter Four

Look At You Now.

then 🐣
now ⭐

Sometimes you don't notice someone growing because you see them little by little.

Then one day, you look at them and realize: they're not that little kid anymore.

You're in Class 12 now, carrying your own dreams, making your own decisions, becoming your own person.

And I couldn't be prouder to have witnessed even a small part of that journey.

Also... who gave you permission to grow up this fast? 😭😂

You make me proud.

Not because you are perfect.

Not because you never make mistakes.

I'm proud because I've watched you keep going.

I've watched you learn, struggle, improve, laugh, make mistakes, try again and slowly become more confident in who you are.

And there is something deeply special about watching a student grow from someone you teach into someone you genuinely root for.

You don't have to become perfect to make me proud. You just have to keep becoming more of yourself.

And every little step you've taken has made me proud to say, "Yes, I taught her once."

And then there's your respect...

Maybe you don't realize how much it means to me when you still remember, still visit, still listen, and still treat me with the same respect.

A teacher can teach hundreds of students. We remember many names. We remember many faces.

But a few students leave something behind that can't be measured by marks, grades or report cards.

You are one of those students.

And honestly, that is one of the sweetest gifts a teacher can receive.

You didn't just learn from me.

You gave me something I didn't know I needed from teaching — the feeling that what I do can stay with someone.

You made me feel heard. You made me feel respected. You made me feel that the time I spent explaining, correcting, encouraging and believing in you actually mattered.

And that means more than any "thank you" could ever explain.

So if you ever wonder whether you were a memorable student...

You absolutely were.

The Letter

Dear Kiddo,

I don't know whether you'll laugh, cry, or make fun of me after reading this — but I wanted to write it anyway.

I met you first as a tiny girl at a birthday, long before I had any idea what you'd become to me. Years passed. I finished school myself, came back to teach, and there you were again — older, but somehow still that same kid I'd noticed once.

You became my student. Then the lessons turned into conversations, and the conversations turned into something I didn't expect: a bond I actually look forward to.

I've watched you grow. I've watched you keep coming back to visit, keep learning, keep treating me with more respect than I probably deserve. And somewhere along the way, you became much more than a student to me.

You're family. You're the little sister life somehow gave me. You're the child I never gave birth to, but somehow got the privilege of loving like one.

Thank you for making me feel like a worthy teacher, sister and a child I never gave birth to but cared the same way.
— Your Sneha
